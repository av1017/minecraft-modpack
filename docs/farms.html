The Farm Stack

Five farms, in the order that actually makes sense — each one either removes a daily chore or feeds directly into the next. Skip nothing here just because it's not exciting; food and iron unlock everything else.

0 / 0 checked
1 Food 2 Iron 3 Sugar Cane 4 Mob XP 5 Villagers
Why this order: food and iron remove the two chores you repeat constantly by hand — eating and re-mining tools. Sugar cane comes next because it feeds paper, which feeds villager trading, which is the actual endgame of "efficient survival." Mob XP and villager breeding are quality-of-life multipliers you add once the essentials are running themselves.
Source: community farm-order consensus, 2026
1

Food Farm — Build This First

The water-flow flood method: no redstone required, works for wheat, carrots, potatoes, and beetroot alike.

Releasing the water at the top sweeps every mature crop down into the hoppers below — the farmland itself is never destroyed.

  • Build a staircase of farmland rows, one water source block placed at the top edge, hidden behind a wall or block.
  • Plant wheat, carrots, potatoes, or beetroot — all behave the same way with this design. Any of these need a light level of 9 or higher at the plant block to grow at all.
  • When crops are mature, release the water — flowing water breaks mature crops and drops their full yield without destroying the farmland, sweeping everything down to a hopper line at the bottom.
  • A lever or simple observer-triggered dispenser can automate the water release, but a manual lever is completely fine for your first version.
  • Once you have surplus food, this directly feeds Farm 5 (villager breeding) later — no separate food source needed for that.

3

Sugar Cane Farm — Build This Third

Feeds paper, which feeds emeralds via librarian trading, which feeds your entire enchanting pipeline. Fully automatable with a simple observer-piston unit.

One thing that changed: the old "zero-tick" instant-growth sugar cane farm trick was patched years ago and does not work in current versions. Any guide describing zero-tick farming is out of date — modern designs rely on natural growth timing with observers.
Source: SyntaxMine, current sugar cane farm design
  • Plant sugar cane next to water — it grows to a maximum of 3 blocks tall and regrows from the base indefinitely once harvested correctly.
  • Place an observer facing each cane stalk from behind, watching for the block to reach its third (top) segment.
  • Wire the observer to a piston placed directly behind the cane — when the observer detects the top block growing in, it pulses the piston to break just the upper section, leaving the base intact to keep growing.
  • Repeat this one observer + one piston per stalk unit side by side to scale up production — it's a simple repeating module, not a single complex machine.
  • Funnel the drops into a hopper line beneath the pistons for full automation with zero manual harvesting.

Mob XP Farm — Build This Fourth

Two real options depending on what you've found — a spawner farm if you're lucky, a dark-room tower if you're not.

Mob farm options compared
DesignRateRequirement
Spawner farm~80 mobs/minRequires finding a dungeon spawner — cheapest, fastest build if you have one
Dark-room towerUp to ~150 mobs/min (4-level sky tower)No spawner needed — needs a fully dark, enclosed platform away from natural caves
Source: XGamingServer, XP farm rate comparison
  • Mobs only spawn at light level 0 on opaque blocks with 2 blocks of clear air above — cover any gaps with slabs or fully enclose the space, or your farm silently underperforms.
  • Build sky platforms high up, away from natural caves and ravines below — competing spawn locations elsewhere reduce what spawns at your farm.
  • Use water streams to push spawned mobs toward a central drop point, funneling them down into a kill chamber.
  • Fall exactly 22 blocks before the kill point — this leaves mobs at half a heart so one hit finishes them for full XP. 24+ blocks kills them outright and gives zero XP — get this number right.
  • You must land the final blow yourself (or hit them within 5 seconds/100 ticks before something else kills them) — mobs give no XP if something else finishes them off.
  • Remember the 128-block mob cap sphere centered on you — stand at your AFK spot within that radius, or spawns elsewhere on the map eat into your farm's budget.
5

Villager Trading Hall & Breeder — Build This Fifth

The capstone. By now you have surplus food and iron to spend on this, and it's what actually sustains your enchanting pipeline long-term.

  • Villagers breed when they have an available bed (one per adult, plus one extra), enough food in their inventory, and are marked "willing" — reached at 3 bread, or 12 of any single crop (carrots/potatoes/beetroot).
  • Bread is the efficient choice — only 3 loaves needed vs. 12 raw crops, which matters a lot once you're running multiple breeding pairs.
  • For full automation: route your food farm's surplus through a hopper to a farmer villager, who converts crops to bread and shares it with nearby villagers automatically — a closed loop that keeps producing babies as long as your food farm runs.
  • A compact, fast breeder: 2 villagers in a 3×3 glass box with a hopper feeding bread in from above — commonly reported at roughly 1 baby every 2-3 minutes once seeded.
  • Move new/excess villagers roughly 32 blocks away from the breeder into your actual trading hall cells — keeps the breeding room from filling up and stalling itself out.
  • This is also where the Enchanting page's trading hall advice plugs in directly — one villager per cell, one workstation per cell, trade once to lock it in.

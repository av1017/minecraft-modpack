BASIC

Manual Mining — Two Real Options

Iron generates in two separate bands. Most guides only mention one of them.

Iron generation, both bands
LocationPeak Y-levelNotes
Underground bandY 16The reliable, always-available option. Y 10–20 keeps you close to peak without excess deepslate digging.
Mountain peak bandY 232The richest band by far: the game tries about 9 times as many iron clusters here (Y 80 and up, peaking at 232) as in the underground band. Exposed on cliff faces of tall mountains and Stony Peaks; in the Cobblemon world, Terralith's mountains reach it easily.
Iron ore veinsDeep (about Y −60 to −8)Rare but huge streaks of iron ore and tuff in the deepslate layer, with solid blocks of raw iron mixed in. If you spot a band of tuff down deep, follow it.
The mountain-peak route is genuinely underrated: because mountain terrain is full of exposed cliff faces, iron ore there is frequently already visible on the surface — no pickaxe-first tunneling required. Testing has shown players gathering 2+ stacks of raw iron in under 10 minutes just by side-hilling across a Stony Peaks biome at the right elevation, without ever placing a torch underground.
Source: Minecraft Wiki, Iron Ore generation (unchanged since 1.18)
  • Underground: branch mine around Y 16 the same way you would for diamond, just much shallower — you'll pass through this band on your way down anyway.
  • Mountain peak: find a Stony Peaks or tall mountain biome and travel sideways across exposed rock faces near the summit rather than tunneling straight in — you'll spot far more ore per block traveled this way.
  • Bring a stack of torches and expect wind/fall risk at altitude — mountain iron is fast but exposes you to long falls in a way underground mining doesn't.
  • Fortune III applies normally to iron ore — worth using once you have it, same as any other Fortune-eligible ore.
INTERMEDIATE

Your First Iron Golem Farm

Once manual mining feels slow, this is where "iron stops being a bottleneck" actually happens. The mechanic: villagers that feel threatened attempt to summon an iron golem for protection, roughly every 5 seconds while panicking.

MINIMUM VILLAGERS3 panicking
Java Edition only needs 3 villagers actively panicking (not just present) to reliably trigger golem spawn attempts.
SPAWN ATTEMPT RATEEvery 5 sec
Per panicking villager, while the panic state is active — this is what a zombie-scare farm is actually built around.
TYPICAL OUTPUT240–400/hr
Iron ingots per hour from a solid single-village farm; some tuned designs report 400–600+.
Source: Minecraft Wiki, Tutorial:Iron golem farming
  • Gather at least 3 villagers (more is safer — see troubleshooting) into an enclosed pen with beds and workstations nearby.
  • Place a caged zombie (behind glass/bars, visible but unable to reach them) near the villager pen — this keeps them permanently panicking, which is what drives the 5-second spawn attempts.
  • Build an open-air spawning platform above the pen, roughly 16×16, since that size hits the practical spawn-rate sweet spot without excess build cost. Keep the space directly above clear.
  • Route spawned golems off the platform (water stream or fall damage-safe drop) into a kill zone — a simple fall onto a half-slab or a manual kill both work; golems have a lot of health, so a fall-based kill needs real height.
  • Each golem killed drops 3–5 iron ingots (average 4) and has roughly a 67% chance of dropping 1–2 poppies.
EXPERT / ABOVE & BEYOND

Zombie-Free Farms & Scaling Up

The panic method works but keeps a zombie loaded near your base permanently, which costs a small amount of performance and requires a name-tagged zombie so it doesn't despawn. There's a cleaner mechanic that skips this entirely.

Both are legitimate, community-verified mechanics. The panic method spawns faster; the gossip method needs no mob, no name tag, and works fine even on Peaceful difficulty.

  • Gossip-based farm: gather 5+ villagers who regularly gossip with each other (this happens naturally when they're grouped and can path to each other) — no zombie required at all. A well-known compact version fits in roughly a 10×7 footprint. (wiki)
  • This design works on Peaceful difficulty too, since it never relies on a hostile mob being present — useful if you or your girlfriend ever toggle difficulty for building sessions.
  • To scale past one village's worth of golems, run multiple separate villager pens feeding into one collection point rather than cramming more villagers into a single pen — golem spawn checks have a claim radius, so overcrowding one pen wastes villagers instead of adding output.
  • Iron golems won't spawn if another golem already exists within 16 blocks in the last 30 seconds, or within 10 blocks of a very recent spawn attempt — spread multiple spawn platforms out accordingly rather than stacking them close together.
REFERENCE

Why Your Farm Isn't Producing

The most common real complaints, matched to the actual mechanic causing them.

Golems spawn but keep falling off the platform.
Known, common defect — golems can spawn right at platform edges. Build a lip/wall around the spawn platform edge so a golem can't wander directly off before your collection system catches it.
Nothing is spawning at all.
Check three things in order: (1) are villagers actually panicking/gossiping, not just standing nearby — presence alone doesn't trigger it; (2) is there already a golem claimed within 16 blocks that's blocking new spawns; (3) do villagers have valid beds and (for the panic method) unobstructed line of sight to the zombie.
My farm's rate dropped after I updated / moved villagers.
Villagers need to have slept in a bed within the last 20 minutes and (per some designs) worked their workstation the previous day to count toward spawn eligibility — moving villagers around resets this state and can silently reduce your effective villager count for a while.
Should I worry about "20 villagers" or "10 villagers + 20 beds" requirements I've read about?
That's the Bedrock Edition mechanic, which spawns golems from full village stats on a per-tick chance — a completely different system. On Java Edition, the panic/gossip mechanics above are what actually matter; ignore Bedrock village-size numbers if you're playing Java.

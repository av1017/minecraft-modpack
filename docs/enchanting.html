The Rush Path — Start Here

If you already have iron, a few diamonds, some lapis, and are around level 15-20, this is the fastest legitimate route to genuinely good gear.

The key fact that reorders everything: Mending is treasure-only — no number of bookshelves or table pulls can ever give it to you. Villager trading is the only renewable source. That means the enchanting table is not your first move; finding a village is.
Source: Minecraft Wiki, Librarian
  1. Find a village. This is the actual bottleneck — everything below depends on it.
  2. Craft a lectern (bookshelf + 4 planks) and place it next to any unemployed villager to make them a Librarian.
  3. Check their first two trades. If neither is what you want, break the lectern and place it again — this resets their entire trade pool for free, instantly.
  4. Repeat until you roll Mending. This costs nothing but a few seconds per attempt — far cheaper than gambling on random table pulls.
  5. Buy it with emeralds (trade paper or other cheap goods to a farmer/villager to get emeralds if you're short).
  6. Put Mending on your pickaxe immediately — from here on, using it repairs it with XP instead of costing durability.
  7. Use your enchanting table (even with 0 bookshelves — it still gives real low-level enchants) for Efficiency and Unbreaking on your diamond pickaxe while you keep working the villager for more books.
  8. Save lapis for the table, not villager trades — villagers only want emeralds.

The Enchanting Table Itself

Useful in parallel with the villager rush above — not a replacement for it.

  • You don't need 15 bookshelves to start — the table gives real enchants at 0 bookshelves too, just at a lower max level. Start using it now, add shelves as you get wood/books spare.
  • Each enchant costs 1-3 lapis and 1-3 XP levels depending on the slot chosen — the "level 30" requirement is a gate to unlock the slot, not the actual price.
  • Enchanting a book instead of a tool directly gives you more flexibility — you can combine books at an anvil later (see Expert tier) rather than committing table rolls straight to your gear.

INTERMEDIATE

Building a Real Trading Hall

Once you're past the rush and want a repeatable book-farming setup instead of one lucky villager.

  • Give each villager their own separate cell with one bed and exactly one workstation — multiple workstations in one cell lets them re-path randomly and overwrite trades you already locked in.
  • Trading with a villager locks that trade forever — even buying a single cheap item locks it in, so trade once you're happy with what's offered, not before.
  • Structure your hall in three parts: an input (where new villagers arrive), cells (where they live/work), and an output (where you actually trade) — keeps the system easy to expand later.
  • Prioritize at minimum one Librarian and one Farmer — Librarian for enchanted books, Farmer for cheap/renewable emeralds to fund those book purchases.
EVERYONE NEEDS ONE

Anvils: Repair, Combine, Rename

An anvil is made from 3 iron blocks on top and 4 iron ingots below in an upside-down T: 31 iron in all. Every job costs XP levels. None of our mods change how anvils work, and the modded armor and weapons (Immersive Armors, Epic Knights) repair and enchant on one like vanilla gear.

When enchantments merge

  • Same enchantment, same level → one level higher, up to the maximum (Sharpness V, Protection IV, Efficiency V…).
  • Same enchantment, different levels → you keep the higher one.
  • Enchantments that can't go together (Sharpness / Smite / Bane of Arthropods; the four Protection types; Fortune / Silk Touch; Mending / Infinity on a bow) → the one from the right slot is dropped.
Every anvil job makes the next one cost more. Each time an item goes through an anvil, its "prior work" cost grows: +0, +1, +3, +7, +15, +31 levels after 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 uses. Both items' costs count. Once a job would cost 40 levels or more, the anvil says Too Expensive! and won't do it, however much XP you have.
  1. Merge books with each other first, then put one finished book on your gear in a single step. Fewer trips through the anvil means a cheaper item.
  2. Put the big ones on early (Mending, Protection IV, Sharpness V) while the item is still cheap to work on.
  3. Get Mending and you'll never repair at the anvil again: XP orbs you pick up repair the item instead.
  4. Rename whenever you like. Renaming on its own doesn't add to the cost.
  5. Hit "Too Expensive!"? A grindstone strips the enchantments (except curses), gives some XP back, and resets the cost, so you can start fresh.
Anvils wear out. Each use has about a 12% chance to damage it: Anvil → Chipped → Damaged → gone. They also fall like sand, and a falling anvil hurts whoever it lands on.
  • Craft an anvil (3 iron blocks + 4 iron ingots)
  • Merge two books into a higher level (like two Sharpness IV into Sharpness V)
  • Put Mending on your main pickaxe or sword
  • Name a tool, or name a pet with a nametag
EXPERT / ABOVE & BEYOND

The Zombie Discount & Anvil Math

Two tricks that dramatically cut the real cost of a fully-enchanted trading hall — most players never learn either one.

Zombie-curing discount: curing a zombie villager (Splash Potion of Weakness + feeding a golden apple) permanently cuts all of that villager's trade prices by 50%. Re-infecting and re-curing the same villager 3-5 times stacks the discount further — a Mending book that would cost ~30 emeralds can drop to roughly ~5 emeralds this way.
Source: Minecraft Wiki, Villager trading hall

Fewer anvil operations on your actual gear means a lower prior-work penalty and avoiding "Too Expensive!"

  • The anvil's prior work penalty doubles roughly every time an item is used in an anvil (cost = 2^uses − 1) — repeatedly enchanting the same tool gets exponentially more expensive.
  • The anvil hard-caps any single operation at 39 levels — past that it refuses with "Too Expensive!" no matter how much XP you have banked.
  • Combine your enchanted books together first, before ever touching your actual tool — this keeps the prior-work penalty on the disposable books, not on the gear you actually care about.
  • Renaming an item at an anvil does not add to its prior work penalty — safe to do anytime.
